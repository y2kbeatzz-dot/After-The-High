Drugs can affect breathing, heart rhythm, temperature, judgment and consciousness. Unknown contents and mixing substances can make the outcome harder to predict.

Fentanyl

Illicit fentanyl can appear in counterfeit pills and other illegal drugs without someone knowing. Opioid overdose can slow or stop breathing.

M30

Cocaine

Cocaine is a stimulant that can sharply raise heart rate and blood pressure and contribute to arrhythmias, seizures, stroke or heart attack.

Meth

Methamphetamine can dangerously increase heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature. Severe effects can include agitation, psychosis, seizures and cardiovascular emergencies.

Heroin & opioids

Opioids can suppress the brain's drive to breathe. Heroin, fentanyl and misused prescription opioids can cause fatal overdose.

Lean / codeine

Codeine is an opioid. Combining opioids with alcohol, benzodiazepines or other depressants can dangerously slow breathing.

Xanax / benzos

Benzodiazepines can cause heavy sedation and impaired coordination. Combining them with opioids or alcohol increases overdose risk.

MDMA

MDMA can raise body temperature and heart rate and affect fluid balance. Products sold as ecstasy or molly may contain unexpected substances.

♥

Ketamine

Ketamine can cause dissociation, impaired awareness and loss of coordination.

Weed / THC

Cannabis can affect attention, short-term memory, coordination, reaction time, mood and judgment. High-THC products such as concentrates, dabs and some edibles can hit much harder than expected and may trigger panic, paranoia or severe confusion in some people. Driving while high is unsafe, and frequent use can become difficult to control for some people.

THC

Vaping / nicotine

Most vapes contain nicotine, which is highly addictive. For children, teens and young adults, nicotine can affect parts of the developing brain involved in attention, learning, mood and impulse control. Vape aerosol is not simply water vapor and can expose the lungs to nicotine, ultrafine particles, flavoring chemicals and other substances.

Alcohol

Alcohol poisoning can suppress breathing and consciousness. Combining it with opioids or sedatives can be especially dangerous.

Unknown pills

Counterfeit pills can look like legitimate medication while containing fentanyl or other unexpected drugs.

DRUG AWARENESS & OVERDOSE PREVENTION

FACTS THAT
CAN SAVE LIVES.

AFTER THE HIGH is a drug awareness website created to make information about addiction, overdose risk, fentanyl, naloxone, and recovery easier to understand. It brings together fentanyl awareness, overdose prevention, drug facts for teens and adults, naloxone information, and addiction recovery resources in one place.

The site covers opioids, counterfeit pills, cocaine, methamphetamine, lean/codeine, benzodiazepines, cannabis/THC, alcohol, MDMA, ketamine, and the risks of mixing substances. It also explains overdose warning signs, why naloxone matters, and where people can find real help.

FENTANYL AWARENESS

Fentanyl can be hidden in other drugs

Illicit fentanyl can appear in counterfeit pills and other illegal drugs without someone knowing. Because fentanyl is a powerful opioid, overdose can happen when breathing becomes dangerously slow or stops.

OVERDOSE PREVENTION

Know the warning signs

Possible opioid overdose signs include being unable to wake, slow or stopped breathing, choking or gurgling sounds, and discolored lips or fingertips. Call 911 immediately if an overdose is suspected.

NALOXONE INFORMATION

Naloxone can reverse an opioid overdose

Naloxone can temporarily reverse an opioid overdose. If it is available, give it, call 911, and stay with the person until emergency help arrives.

ADDICTION RECOVERY RESOURCES

Finding help should be easier

The resource section links to crisis support, treatment locators, poison control, naloxone information, and other official services so people can move from awareness to real help.

DRUG FACTS FOR TEENS

Simple facts without glamorizing use

The goal is to explain what different substances can do to breathing, heart rate, judgment, memory, coordination, temperature, and consciousness in clear language that is easy to understand.

COUNTERFEIT PILLS

A pill can look real and still be dangerous

Counterfeit pills may look like legitimate medication while containing fentanyl or other unexpected substances. Appearance alone cannot confirm what is actually in a pill.

VAPING & NICOTINE AWARENESS · KIDS · TEENS · PARENTS · ADULTS

VAPING ISN'T
JUST WATER VAPOR.

Vaping belongs in drug and addiction awareness too. E-cigarettes and vapes can contain nicotine, and nicotine is highly addictive. Youth vaping matters because the brain continues developing into young adulthood, and nicotine can affect attention, learning, mood and impulse control.

This section is here for young people who already vape, kids and teens who are being pressured to try it, parents and caregivers who are worried, educators, and adults trying to quit. The goal is information and support — not shame.

NICOTINE DEPENDENCE

Nicotine can create cravings and withdrawal

Most vapes contain nicotine. With repeated use, the brain and body can become used to nicotine, making it harder to go without vaping and causing cravings or withdrawal when someone tries to stop.

YOUTH & DEVELOPING BRAINS

Kids and teens are especially important to protect

Nicotine can affect parts of the adolescent brain involved in attention, learning, mood and impulse control. No tobacco product, including e-cigarettes, is considered safe for children, teens or young adults.

WHAT'S IN THE AEROSOL?

It is not simply harmless water vapor

Vape aerosol can contain nicotine, ultrafine particles, flavoring chemicals and other substances. What someone inhales can vary by product, device and liquid.

FLAVORS & SOCIAL PRESSURE

Why young people may start

Flavored products, marketing, social influence, curiosity, stress and nicotine dependence can all play a role in starting or continuing to vape.

THC VAPES

Not every vape contains nicotine

Some devices contain THC or other substances instead of nicotine. Contents and strength can be difficult to judge, especially with informal or counterfeit products.

QUITTING

Needing help to quit is not unusual

Quitting nicotine can be difficult because dependence and withdrawal are real. Free quitlines, text programs, counseling and other support are available.

For kids, teens, parents & schools

If a young person is vaping, support works better than humiliation. Talk clearly about nicotine and addiction, make it easier to ask for help, and connect them with a pediatrician, school health professional, quit program or other trusted support when needed.

COMMON QUESTIONS

DRUG AWARENESS
FAQ.

What is fentanyl awareness?

Fentanyl awareness means understanding that illicit fentanyl can appear in counterfeit pills and other illegal drugs, recognizing opioid overdose warning signs, and knowing that naloxone can reverse an opioid overdose.

What are common signs of an opioid overdose?

Signs can include being unable to wake, very slow or stopped breathing, choking or gurgling sounds, and discolored lips or fingertips. If an overdose is suspected, call 911 immediately.

What does naloxone do?

Naloxone can temporarily reverse the effects of an opioid overdose. It does not replace emergency care, so 911 should still be called and the person should not be left alone.

Where can someone find addiction recovery resources?

AFTER THE HIGH links to official crisis, treatment, poison control, naloxone, and substance-use resources in the Help & Resources section.

Is this a drug facts website for teens too?

Yes. The information is written in plain language so teens, young adults, families, educators, and anyone looking for drug awareness information can understand the risks without glamorizing drug use.

Is vaping safe for kids or teens?

No tobacco product, including e-cigarettes, is considered safe for children, teens or young adults. Most vapes contain nicotine, which is highly addictive and can affect the developing brain.

Is vape aerosol just water vapor?

No. Vape aerosol can contain nicotine, ultrafine particles, flavoring chemicals and other substances. The exact contents vary by product and liquid.

Can someone become addicted to vaping?

Yes. Nicotine is highly addictive, and regular use can lead to cravings, withdrawal symptoms and difficulty stopping.

What should a teen do if they want to quit vaping?

They can use free support such as Smokefree Teen or Truth Initiative's EX Program, talk with a trusted adult or healthcare professional, and use quitline or counseling support.

What can parents do if their child is vaping?

Start with a calm conversation, focus on health and nicotine dependence instead of punishment, ask what makes quitting difficult, and connect the young person with a healthcare professional or youth quit program when needed.

Are THC vapes the same as nicotine vapes?

No. Some vapes contain THC or other substances instead of nicotine. Contents and potency can vary, and informal or counterfeit cartridges can add additional uncertainty.
