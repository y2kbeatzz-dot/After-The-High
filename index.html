Know what
you're facing.

Drugs can affect breathing, heart rhythm, temperature, judgment and consciousness. Unknown contents and mixing substances can make the outcome harder to predict.

Fentanyl

Illicitly manufactured fentanyl is extremely potent and can appear in counterfeit pills and other illegal drugs without someone knowing. Opioid overdose can slow or stop breathing.

Cocaine

Cocaine is a powerful stimulant. It can sharply raise heart rate and blood pressure and contribute to arrhythmias, seizures, stroke or heart attack. Illicit cocaine may also contain fentanyl.

Meth

Methamphetamine can dangerously increase heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature. Severe effects can include agitation, psychosis, seizures and cardiovascular emergencies.

Heroin & opioids

Opioids can suppress the brain's drive to breathe. Heroin, fentanyl and misused prescription opioids can cause fatal overdose, especially when combined with other depressants.

Lean / codeine

Codeine is an opioid. Prescription cough mixtures may also contain other sedating ingredients. Combining opioids with alcohol, benzodiazepines or other depressants can dangerously slow breathing.

Xanax / benzos

Benzodiazepines can cause heavy sedation and impaired coordination. Combining them with opioids or alcohol increases the risk of severe respiratory depression and overdose.

MDMA

MDMA can raise body temperature and heart rate and affect fluid balance. Products sold as ecstasy or molly may contain unexpected substances.

Ketamine

Ketamine can cause dissociation, impaired awareness and loss of coordination. High exposure or mixing with other depressants can increase danger.

Alcohol

Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant. Alcohol poisoning can suppress breathing and consciousness. Combining it with opioids or sedatives can be especially dangerous.

Unknown pills

Counterfeit pills can look like legitimate medication while containing fentanyl or other unexpected drugs. Appearance alone cannot tell you what is inside.

Overdose signs

Possible opioid overdose signs include inability to wake, slow or stopped breathing, choking or gurgling sounds, and discolored lips or fingertips. Treat suspected overdose as an emergency.

Naloxone saves lives

Naloxone can reverse an opioid overdose. Give it if available, call 911, support breathing when appropriate, place the person on their side to reduce choking risk, and stay with them until help arrives.

Mixing changes everything.

Using multiple substances can make effects unpredictable. Stimulants do not cancel opioid overdose risk, and combinations of depressants can compound breathing suppression.

LSD & psilocybin

Inhalants

Cannabis / THC

Prescription stimulants
