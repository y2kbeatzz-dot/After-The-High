OVERDOSE AWARENESS

OVERDOSE.

An overdose can become life-threatening quickly. Knowing warning signs and responding fast can save a life.

Possible opioid overdose signs

Someone may be difficult or impossible to wake, may have very slow or stopped breathing, or may make choking or gurgling sounds. Lips or fingertips can appear pale, gray, or blue depending on skin tone.

What to do

Call 911. Give naloxone if opioid overdose is suspected and naloxone is available. Stay with the person and follow emergency instructions until help arrives.

Naloxone

Naloxone can temporarily reverse the effects of opioids and restore breathing. More than one dose may sometimes be needed.

Mixing substances

Combining opioids with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or other depressants can increase the risk of dangerously slowed breathing.
